Hello and welcome to the second issue of Andrew Magazine, the periodical covering the life and thoughts of me, Andrew Kraemer. This week I spent the holidays with Kate's family in Canton, TX. I was feeling ambitious and planned to release my FOTY list -- Favorites of the Year (listing out my favorite things of the year regardless if it came out this year or not). Unfortunately, the constant parties, Tim Allen movies, and headbutts from my four year old nephew ensured I did nothing productive the whole week.

### Christmas Movies
Over the past few years, I've been a bit of a Scrooge when it comes to Christmas. Yes, seeing family and spending quality time is wonderful and should be cherished, but I've never been particularly into the hyper commercialization of the holiday. Also, the trees, the songs, the mediocre movies always struck me as a lot of pomp for not very much circumstance.

This year, I said screw it. Yes, Christmas is a hyper commercialized mess. Yes, there are parts I wish I could opt out of. But after thinking about it I realized Christmas is it like a month of acting like we're in a Broadway musical. Christmas is [camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_(style)), and I love camp.

When you're full of roast beast, and you are looking for any way for your nephew to stop headbutting you. A Christmas movie is the best way to recharge before going for another round of sausage balls and eggnog. Below is the list of Christmas movies we watched this year. Also, if you want to see my list of favorite Christmas movies see the list [here](https://letterboxd.com/kidman007/list/christmas-i-like/).

#### It's a Wonderful Life
I mean, come on. This is a stone cold classic. While I'm not sure why we had to watch it on TV with cialis commercials interrupting the flow of the movie when I know for a fact Kate's family owns the DVD, It's hard to not get sucked in by Jimmy Stewart any time he is on the screen. I didn't grow up with this one -- unlike seemingly every other person on the planet, but better late than never.

#### Four Christmases
This was a recommendation from one of Kate's friends, self proclaimed lover of trash movies. Vince Vaughn and Reese Witherspoon play a long term couple with no plans to marry or have kids. They both come from broken families. Why continue the cycle when we can just have fun with each other? Every year they develop elaborate reasons to opt out of visiting their parents on Christmas, but this year they get busted and have to go and visit each of their four parents.

This is one I had never seen, and my expectations were low. But I had an absolute blast with this one. Is it great? Not really. But how could you not love a movie that has one of the all time greatest lines in cinema history:

> "I don't want to speak ill of your mother on Christmas, but she's nothing but a common street whore"

This one is going into my rewatch list.

#### Christmas with the Kranks
This was another recommendation from the trash lover. This movie was baaaaaaaad. Tim Allen and Jamie Lee Curtis decide to opt out of Christmas and plan on going on a Carribean cruise. The neighbors in town do not take this well. I don't want to dwell on it, but the choices every single character makes is totally crazy and not relatable.

#### The Santa Clause
I hadn't seen this one since I was a kid and it absolutely slaps. The lighting and cinematography is bananas. When people say that movies don't look like they used to, they are talking about movies like this. We watched this right before Elf, and it's clear to me that this movie walked so Elf could run.

Tim Allen is so much better in this movie than Christmas with the Kranks it is insane. It makes sense to me how Tim Allen is able to continue printing money given roles like this.

#### Elf
Elf is obviously amazing. The jokes are so tight and Will Ferrell is perfectly cast. I had a falling out with this movie over the past couple of years. I was the target audience when it came out and it is every millennial's favorite Christmas movie. I just got sick of it. However, it had been a long enough time since I had last seen it and it really is the perfect background movie.

#### Muppet Christmas Carol
I haven't seen this one since I was a kid, and it is certainly going back into the list. We only watched the first 20 min, but I know that if you put Michael Caine with Muppets you're going get something special.

#### National Lampoons Christmas Vacation
I think this movie is bad. I didn't grow up with it. I'm sure it's foundational to a lot of comedy films, but it just doesn't work for me.

#### Family Stone
This is a favorite of my mother-in-law. It's really cute and has an absolutely dynamite cast.

#### Carol
Okay, this one is debatable as a Christmas movie. It takes place during Christmas, so I'm counting it in my book. It's also called Carol!

I have been working my way through the [NYT Top 100 Movies of the 21st Century](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/movies/best-movies-21st-century.html). I've been working through the 24 I hadn't seen, [and I've got nine movies left](https://letterboxd.com/kidman007/list/remaining-nyt-top-100/). Most of them are either depressing, foreign films, or depressing foreign films. Carol was recently on the list until I was able to catch it playing at the Alamo Drafthouse near my house.

The year is 1952 in Manhattan. Cate Blanchett is a wealthy socialite who is divorcing her husband because of her continued romantic relationships with women. Rooney Mara is a young department store clerk. After a chance encounter, they begin meeting more regularly and things get complicated.

Before the NYT list, I had never heard of this movie, and I was absolutely gobsmacked by how good it was. Every scene looks like [Nighthawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nighthawks_%28Hopper%29), and all I want to do is drink Martinis and smoke cigarettes with Cate Blanchett. While the story leaves a lot to interpretation on what the characters will do after the film, it was refreshing watching a movie with LGBTQ+ characters who **do not** meet a sad ending.
