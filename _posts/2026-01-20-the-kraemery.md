For our first anniversary, I got Kate an ice cream Kitchenaid attachment and a copy of [Jeni's Splendid Ice Creams at Home](https://jenis.com/products/jenis-splendid-ice-creams-at-home). I didn't realize it at the time, but what I thought was a thoughtful gift for my wife was actually a thoughtful gift for myself. I've used the ice cream maker a few times, but not enough to really build any real ice cream expertise.

Over the the years there have been a handful of foods I've wanted to dive into in a more intentional way. I've wanted to smoke meats, make ice cream, and make pies. I also really want to get into pickling. The problem with all of these delicious treats is if I make them all of the time, I'll become fat and die.

I had an epiphany. What if, instead of making ice cream for myself, I made it for other people? What if, instead of bringing a bottle of wine to a friend's dinner (which has a decent chance of being a bottle of wine they brought to my house), I brought a custom, hand made, sweet treat that everyone loves? And what if I had a sticker with a cool design, so people know I made it? And so, The Kraemery was born.

The logo, courtesy of [Nano Banana](https://gemini.google/overview/image-generation/), is borrowed from the style of my hometown ice cream shop, [Homer's](https://www.homersicecream.com/). I remember one year of little league baseball my dad was a coach. We won our first game, and my dad declared that every time we won, he'd take the whole team to Homer's for ice cream. We never won again.

So far, I've made two flavors: olive oil and graham central station. 

Part of my immediate goals is to figure out what goes into a base that I like with ice cream. The Graham Central batch came out a little cakey. The Olive Oil batch feels a little too buttery to me. You can use gums, like xanthan or locust, or eggs to change the consistency. 

Part of my new years resolution is to be more intentionally creative. It's hard to create. Every creation is a reflection of the effort you put in -- not just in the single output but of all the work you've put into a discipline. Sometimes we experiment, and sometimes we practice. Ice cream makes other people happy, and it especially makes me happy. I'm so excited now that the Kraemery is open.

I wish good movies came out all year -- instead of just Oscar season. Of course a lot of good ones are sprinkled throughout but the end of the year is usually when we get all of the bangers. I mean, last time I talked about Marty Supreme and now I've got three more to talk about? Amazing!

#### No Other Choice
I'm going to say to go and see all of the movies I mention here, but I really think that No Other Choice is the one you should see with the most urgency.

It's a story about a man who is laid off as an engineer at a paper factory and is willing to do anything to get one of the few open positions that he is perfectly qualified for -- even murder.

Last year, via my [favorite movie podcast](https://www.blankcheckpod.com/), I watched most of director Park's movies. The podcast hosts were extremely warm on his whole filmography, whereas not every film he's done works for me. The highly lauded movies from his "Revenge Trilogy" (Sympathy for Mr. Vengeance, Oldboy, Lady Vengeance) would bum me out so much I couldn't appreciate any of the craft he is so well known for. On the other hand, many of his newer movies have me leaning forward in anticipation the whole time, like The Handmaiden and Decision to Leave. Now (without question) No Other Choice is my favorite movie of his.

No Other Choice keeps you off balance the whole movie. You don't know if you should be rooting for or against the main character. Men are constantly making decisions that negatively impact the people around them and justify it saying they have "no other choice". On top of the deep social criticisims and character studies, the film is absolutely gorgeous. There is a shot where the characters drink a "bomb shot" and somehow the camera is inside of the glass they are drinking out of. Phenomenal.

Somehow the film has gotten **zero** Oscar nominations. It was even snubbed for foreign film. Despite that, it's certainly one of my favorite films of the year and an awesome watch in the theater.

#### 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple
I don't know what else to say other than go see 28 Years Later on Netflix as soon as possible and go and see The Bone Temple in Theaters before it leaves. Ralph Fiennes, who has always been spectacular, has become one of my favorite actors as a result of these two movies.

I went back and watched 28 Days Later -- the first in this series -- which I hadn't seen since high school. While 28 Days is still a great movie, I feel like it doesn't hit quite as hard now as it did when it invented the fast zombie genre. On the other hand, directors Danny Boyle and Nia DaCosta dig into more interesting questions than its predecessor: What is the value of life at the end of the world? What does it mean to honor the dead? What are the value of principals when there are no structures to enforce them?

Also there is a zombie with a massive dong.

#### Avatar: Fire and Ash
Listen, you probably think Avatar is lame. I understand where you're coming from. It's a story about blue people on a planet called Pandora -- I mean come on. For a movie that has made over $1.5 billion dollars, it is shocking how small the cultural footprint of this franchise is. All I have to say is that I think that people are forgetting that this is a movie made by James Cameron when he has basically unlimited money. How could it possibly not be at the very least an absolute blast.

I went last week to Houston for a work trip. I checked into my hotel in the afternoon and had the evening free to myself, so I got a margarita at Papacitos and then went to go see the new Avatar. There were children running in the aisles, and there were people in the theater talking too loud. When it started I also was questioning my decision to see the movie. "Here come the blue freaks", I thought to myself. Three hours later I was absolutely gripped. I wanted to jump out of my seat and shout, "I see you!"
