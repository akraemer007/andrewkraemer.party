Listen. I knew that the flu was supposed to be bad, but I think I really lacked the imagination to understand how bad it would be.

As much as we may try, I believe we -- humans in general -- have poor imaginations. Sometimes a friend of mine will have a baby or get a puppy, and they'll share with me the difficulties and exhaustion that they are experiencing. They'll say something like, "I never knew I could be this tired."

"Well, I knew you could be this tired," I think cleverly to myself. I've heard how tiring having a kid will be and when I have a kid, I won't complain about how tired I am.

I felt the same way about the flu. In my mind the flu conjures my parent's old home, the price is right, and -- most importantly -- getting to skip school. Sure, I'm feeling crummy, but I get the house to myself, and I get to do whatever I want. The flu I remember was almost a like a vacation.

Fast forward to this week, where I slept 40 hours in two days. When I was awake, I was so ill I couldn't even have Braveheart playing in the background. I couldn't talk. My breathing would break and choke like a garbage disposal working through a particularly dense set of food. My fever was 103 F. As I would lie in bed, struggling for each breath and alternating between sweating and shivering, I would dance between dream and reality.

In the dream there were no real characters that I could remember, but there were books. There were books everywhere as far as the eye could see. I could never keep track of what I needed to do with the books, but I knew that something needed to be done. When I would awake, I'd try to hold onto the dream. The circular logic of the dream was easier than the reality of the flu.

Anyway, seven days later and I'm fine now. I'm still weak, but I'm going on walks and doing chores now. My big take away here is if you think you have the flu, go and get tested. Go and get drugs. The flu is worse than you can imagine; you'll be tired as having a baby.

Days two and three of the sickness were far and away the most intense, so I've had some time to do non-fever-dream activities.

I spent a lot of my time playing Blue Prince. It's a first person puzzle game like Myst -- if you remember that from the old 90's PC gaming days. In Blue Prince you are both solving the puzzles of a mysterious manor, but each day the layout of the manor changes, and you get to design the new layout each day. To win, you need to place the right rooms in the right order based on randomness, while also keeping all of the interconnected puzzles and the rules of the house you've learned as you've played.

I've filled a notebook half full of notes from my runs. I'm through 45 runs of the game, and still haven't achieved a win state yet, but I think I've collected a lot of information about the various sub mysteries. I'm obsessed with this game, but where I am at I am hitting a tension between the random elements of the game and my desire to push the puzzles I have solved to good use. I thought for a while I would "fully beat" this game. Now, at this point, I'm just trying to get a simple win before deciding if I'm going deeper.
