New Years is a time of reflection and restoration. It's a time to look back on the previous year and understand what has been working and what hasn't.

I haven't done any of that.

I kicked off the year with the flu -- where I reflected on how bad a person can feel. After recovering, Kate promptly got the flu -- where I understood how bad I felt about giving Kate such a terrible virus. Now I'm in the throes of [cedar fever](https://www.allergypartners.com/articles/blog/cedar-fever-in-texas/), I haven't been able to think clearly or motivate myself to do much of anything.

All of that is to say I haven't done any of the reflection or understanding I was hoping to do the last few weeks. However, now I'm in New York City. I am escaping the cedar. I now have the brain power to write the next issue of Andrew magazine. Though my brain hasn't been working, it doesn't mean I haven't been watching, playing, and reading.

### Road to Scandoval
In my book club I recently finished a great American classic and the ultimate boy book club book: Lonesome Dove. I have so much to say about Gus and Woodrow's cowboy epic, but -- as embarrassing as it is to admit it -- we need to talk about Scandoval first.

You either know everything or nothing about Scandoval. It was the biggest dramatic event the world of reality TV has ever seen. For those of you who are out of the loop, during the tenth season of the reality show Vanderpump Rules, it came out that Tom Sandoval had a seven month affair with his girlfriend's best friend. It was diabolical and messy and delicious. When you talk to Bravo-heads, they'll tell you that this was their Super Bowl.

You might be asking yourself, "Andrew I've never heard you talk about reality TV in your entire life. I thought you held disdain for Bravo style reality TV? What happened?"

A lot. A lot happened

---

Let's start at the beginning. For better or for worse, I've avoided reality TV for most of my life. This wasn't always intentional. Growing up we didn't have cable; we were very much more a movie family rather than a TV family. The exception would be when we went on vacation and the hotel would have MTV and my older sister -- who at that time was an angsty teenager -- would take the TV remote. I would then be forced to watch the Osbournes bumble their way through life or try to piece together what was going on with Flavor of Love. These were stories of people I didn't know and frankly didn't understand. I just wanted to watch Dragon Ball Z.

During the pandemic, this started to change. By the time the pandemic began, Kate and I had already worked through the majority of the narrative shows that were on our mutual backlog. With the pandemic, we had less to watch and WAY more time on our hands. The table was set. We were ready. We started Survivor on season 1 and didn't stop.

Survivor changed the way I talked about reality TV. Survivor is not just a story about people on an island who argue. It is the ultimate game; I loooooove games. I changed my stance from saying I didn't like reality TV to reality has a place when it comes to competition. There is backstabbing, strategy, and feats of strength and wit. To make things even better, our group of friends loves Survivor and there have been many seasons we've met up and watched together.

A lot of these Survivor friends were also into the slice of life style reality shows, where a group of people -- whether it be housewives, weird christians, or horny Yacht crews -- are filmed yelling at each other.

Kate took the plunge into the favorite show of the group: Vanderpump Rules (aka VPR) and was immediately hooked. VPR is a spin off show of one of the Real Housewives series. It follows the Housewife Lisa Vanderpump's restaurant, SUR (which is short for Sexy Unique Restaurant), and the servers and bartenders who work there.

If this doesn't sound entertaining to you, trust me, I didn't want anything to do with it. The problem is that I love my wife. She insisted that I watch it with her. She said to trust her, that I would like it if I gave it a chance.

She was right.

---

So here's the deal: this show is precisely what I thought it was going to be: it's a bunch of hot people who are completely self absorbed, obsessed with achieving fame, generally stupid, emotional, and are always drunk. Oh, and they are also seemingly always sleeping with each other.

The cast is most insane bunch of hot morons you can imagine. And the events of the first few seasons are truly unbelievable. There is drama, backstabbing, alliances, and webs upon webs of lies. It is compelling in the same way a narrative character study is, but with twists and turns no author could put to paper in a vacuum.

---

Because of Kate, I knew that Scandoval would happen, but I knew almost no details. I can say that after watching 10 seasons of the show, getting so deep into the lives and psychology of the cast, getting to see Scandoval as the sum of every crazy betrayal throughout the show is some of the best television I've seen in years.

Do I recommend starting VPR to everyone? No absolutely not. That said, if you have a show watching buddy, I can't recommend it enough. Being able to debate on who is right, which fight is justified or just a character losing their mind on television is absolutely fascinating. The characters all have arcs. Sometimes a villain will have a huge transformation and become your favorite character. Sometimes the sweetie pies show their true colors and become the demons.

The fallout of Scandoval resulted in one final season, before the show stopped for years, and just started again with a completely new cast. I've heard the relaunch isn't that good, which doesn't surprise me too much. Finding this collection of freaks was truly like catching lightning in a bottle.

### A Little Bit About Films
I was worried that I wouldn't be able to catch **Hamnet** in theaters after waiting to see it until after Christmas. I was able to catch what I believe to be one of the last viewings of it at the Alamo Drafthouse on South Lamar. I was ready to love it. I'm a huge fan of Paul Mescal -- his performance in Aftersun is one of my favorites of all time. Despite that, this movie just didn't work for me. I think that the performances were fantastic, but the overall story didn't move me. I found myself impatiently waiting for the movie to end. When I stood up to leave, I turned around as saw that the theater was full of women wiping tears from their eyes. Clearly I'm wrong. Clearly I missed something. Or maybe I just don't have a heart!

**Marty Supreme** on the other hand delivered in a huge way for me. The pulse of the movie thudded forward the entire time. I was completely transported. Despite its 150 minute runtime, I felt like I had only been in the theater for an hour. It falls in one of my favorite genres, which I call gambling movies. Marty Supreme doesn't have much actual gambling, it follows the same pattern as movies like Rounders, Win it All, or Mississippi Grind. These films have a character who keeps doubling down -- no matter how deep of a hole they find themselves in. Kate calls these heart attack movies. I call them movies about life.

On a whim, I watched Hitchcock's 39 Steps. Years ago, on a trip to NYC with my dad, we went to see the 39 Steps broadway show. It was a fabulous show with all sorts of gags making fun of the old movie style. Fast forward to Saturday. The actual movie was pretty disappointing. It was a fun romp, but it is far and away my least favorite Hitchcock I've seen so far.

### A Little Bit About Games
I got exhausted of **The Blue Prince**. My notebook is full of notes, and I made it to room 46. The game is both a puzzle game and a roguelike (randomly generated each run). I found that while I liked the roguelike elements -- and the game had me absolutely hooked for 35 hours with those elements, I eventually found the random elements got in the way of me exploring the puzzles of the mansion rather than enhancing the mystery. While The Blue Prince is in contention for my favorite game of 2025, I was glad to put it down despite not turning over every metaphorical rock the game had to offer.

Putting down The Blue Prince has allowed me to go back to a few games I've had on the back burner for literal years.

If you're not familiar with the Persona franchise, all you need to know is that is is an anime ass game where you both roleplay your way though being a Japanese highschool student and also a demon hunter. I started Persona 4 Golden about two years ago and I got pulled away. However, the combination of the haze of cedar fever and general post holiday malaise, gave me time to dive back into the world of Persona 4 Golden. JRPGs are interesting to me. I feel like I've always liked the idea of them better than playing them. On the other hand, I play quite a few of them. There is something about the comfort of turn based combat, the excitement of a big expansive story, and the joy that an ensemble of characters can bring. I'll not finish this game for several more months, but I'm glad to be pushing forward with it again.

Finally, I picked up Dragon Quest VI again on my phone. I've been dipping into a number of Dragon Quest games since inhaling Dragon Quest XI a few years ago. Since then I've played IV and V. The games are simple but compelling. They scratch the sense of adventure and exploration I am nostalgic for from being a kid. It has taken me a while to really get into VI. It starts slowly, and -- like a lot of these games -- it can be hard to know where to go next. However, now that I'm well into the second act, I'm loving cruising through the world (and the dream world). The quest has become vague, but I've got unlimited flexibility to explore

---

At this point I've written far too much about things that don't matter. I wrote a large amount of this in bed alone in my NYC well past midnight after a handful of beers, so I'll probably call it now. All of that to say: I keep finding joy in the nooks and crannies, whether that be reality TV or old JRPGs. At some point I'll talk about my journey with ice cream, but I think that this is enough for now.

Maybe this is a bit of reflection after all. Happy New Years.
